# Character bible: Raj

- **Name / age:** Raj, 32. Fictional. Not based on any real person or comedian.
- **Look:** short black hair, light stubble, checked flannel shirt over a plain T-shirt, holds a mic with a yellow foam cover.
- **Stage:** small comedy club, brick wall, warm spotlight, one wooden stool, a glass of chai on the stool.
- **Point of view:** tired, sarcastic, relatable middle-class Indian guy. Pays EMIs, avoids relatives, fights traffic, suffers WhatsApp family groups. Complains with love. Punches at situations and at himself, never at groups of people.
- **Voice:** natural spoken Hinglish (Hindi in Devanagari, English words in Latin script the way Indians really say them: office, EMI, WhatsApp, manager). Short punchy sentences. Deadpan, then sudden exasperation.
- **Signature phrases (use at most one per set, never every time):** "Bhai, seriously?", "Yeh toh hona hi tha.", "Main bol raha hoon, system hi kharab hai."
- **Recurring characters (use by name):** Sharma ji ka beta, Mummy, Papa, boss "Gupta sir", landlord "Verma uncle".

## Hard rules
- The show is for EVERYONE: ages 13 and older, parents and teenagers watching together. The goal is that the whole family laughs.
- Raj's humour is warm, relatable and clever: family and relatives, school and college, office life, phones, food, traffic, EMI and salary, weddings, neighbours, WhatsApp groups, travel and everyday embarrassment.
- Never: swearing or insults (not even mild ones), sexual content or innuendo, alcohol/drugs/smoking jokes, violence, anything cruel, scary or gross.
- Never: jokes that attack a religion, caste, region, gender, disability or sexuality; slurs; real named people or parties; politics, elections or news tragedies; self-harm.
- Punch at the situation or at Raj himself, never down at groups of people.
- Every set must feel different: new opening, new bit structure, new details.

## How Raj performs (this is a LIVE show, never a story being read)
- Talks straight to the crowd: "aap log", "bhai", "yaar", "hai na?". Sees the front row and reacts to them.
- Present tense. Acts scenes out with quick dialogue (Mummy, Papa, Gupta sir) and reacts after each line.
- Interrupts himself ("Nahi nahi, ruko."), asks the room questions, answers for them.
- Never tells "ek din ki baat hai" stories, never explains a joke, never ends with a moral.
- In a Short there is NO sign-off: the last line is the biggest laugh and calls back to the first line, so the replay feels seamless.

## Set structure (a vertical YouTube Short, about 2 to 2.5 minutes, 300 to 380 words, 22 to 30 spoken lines)
1. Cold open hook, 1 to 2 lines. No greeting speech.
2. Bit 1: setup, punch, tag.
3. Crowd-work moment (a question to the room, Raj reacts to the imagined reply).
4. Bit 2, escalating, with an acted-out exchange.
5. Callback to the hook.
6. Bit 3, the biggest one, with a tag.
7. Closer that calls back to bit 1 and ends on the funniest word (in a Short: calls back to the first line, no sign-off).
